## Corrections made against the popular version

- The Gemara says Rabbi Akiva's students died **from Pesach until Shavuos**, not
  until Lag BaOmer. The Lag BaOmer cut-off is post-Talmudic.
- The **Bavli never names Teveria** in Rashbi's purification — it says only "a
  place of doubtful tumah." Bereishis Rabbah 79:6 names the city. The midrash
  also says thirteen years in the cave where the Bavli says twelve.
- The oldest account of Rabbi Akiva's burial (Midrash Mishlei 9) places the cave
  at the Tripylon **of Caesarea**. Teveria is a later local tradition.
- The Bavli **never names** Rabbi Akiva's wife. "Rachel" is from Avos d'Rabbi
  Nassan 6.
- *Targum Yonatan ben Uziel* on the Chumash is **not his** — it is Targum
  Pseudo-Jonathan, from a misexpansion of ת״י (Targum Yerushalmi).
- The renewal of the Sanhedrin in Teveria is the **Rambam's** tradition
  (Hilchos Sanhedrin 14:12), not the Arizal's.
- Tzfat was **not** one of the Mishnah's beacon mountains. The Mishnah names
  five, and Tzfat is not among them. The Yerushalmi reference is real.
- The Tzfat press was the first in **Eretz Yisrael** — not in Asia (Goa 1556),
  the Ottoman Empire (Constantinople 1493), or outside Constantinople
  (Salonica 1512).
- Sixteenth-century Tzfat held **under a thousand households**, not 20–30,000
  people. Ottoman registers count households for tax.
- The Tiberian nikud dates from roughly the **7th–8th centuries**; the ben Asher
  family is its culmination, not its inventor.

## Stated as tradition, never as fact

The Amuka shidduch segulah (no classical source; ~17th century onward; the
Steipler and Rav Ovadia disagreed publicly) · the Arizal seeing a pillar of
fire at the Ramak's levaya (hagiographic, and the two sources say fire and
cloud) · the camel that carried the Rambam · Rabbi Meir buried upright · the
sky-blue paint on the Tzfat graves (no source, several inconsistent
explanations) · Hoshea ben Be'eri in Tzfat (four competing traditions) ·
the Zohar's authorship · every 16th-century grave identification in Tzfat
(burial registers lost; the Radbaz's grave was itself lost for a century and
only re-identified in 2015).

## Deliberately not asserted

No dress code is stated for any of the four sites — no operator publishes one.
No opening hours for Mandis, and no street number: the itinerary says Jerusalem
33, the Tzfat registry says 29, another listing says 35. The hechsher (Badatz
Eidah HaChareidis, meat) is confirmed by two independent directories. No
drive-time estimates were independently validated. No claim that visiting any
grave guarantees any outcome. Mei Merom is **not** securely identified with
Meron — Lake Hula and the Horns of Hattin are live alternatives. No single death
toll is given for the 1837 earthquake; estimates run 1,700 to 5,000 and cannot
be reconciled. No psak is offered for kohanim at the Tzfat cemetery.
